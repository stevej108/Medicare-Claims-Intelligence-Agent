# Analytical Limitations

## Synthetic Data

This project uses CMS DE-SynPUF synthetic Medicare-style claims
data.

The data do not represent actual Medicare beneficiaries.

Results derived from the dataset should therefore be interpreted
as demonstrations of analytical methodology rather than estimates
of real Medicare utilization, disease prevalence, healthcare
spending, or outcomes.

## No Population-Level Medicare Inference

Statistics generated by the claims intelligence system should not
be generalized to the real Medicare population.

Examples include:

- healthcare utilization rates
- chronic-condition prevalence
- healthcare cost distributions
- high-cost-member prevalence
- longitudinal utilization patterns
- member segment sizes

These describe only the synthetic analytical population.

## Analytical Cost Definition

The project's total_annual_cost field is a composite analytical
measure defined as:

MEDREIMB_IP
+ MEDREIMB_OP
+ MEDREIMB_CAR
+ rx_total_cost

This measure was created to support analytical comparison within
the project.

It should not be interpreted as a complete actuarial measure of
Medicare spending, total cost of care, allowed amount, or total
healthcare expenditure.

## Claims Reimbursement Reconciliation

Detailed inpatient claim payments were compared with annual
beneficiary-summary Medicare inpatient reimbursement.

The measures reconciled closely but did not match exactly for
every beneficiary-year.

The beneficiary-summary reimbursement fields are therefore used
as the primary annual reimbursement measures in the analytical
member-year dataset.

## Missing Claim Dates

A small number of institutional claims contain insufficient date
information for analytical-year attribution.

These records are preserved at the source level but excluded from
analyses that require beneficiary-year assignment.

Missing analytical year should not be interpreted as zero
utilization.

## Cross-Year Claims

Some institutional claims span calendar years.

Claims that begin before the analytical observation window but
end within the observation window are attributed to the in-window
completion year.

The analysis does not prorate utilization or financial amounts
across calendar years.

## Chronic Conditions

The chronic-condition indicators are synthetic CMS beneficiary
summary variables.

The project counts eleven available chronic-condition indicators
to create chronic_condition_count.

This count is an analytical measure of documented condition burden
within the synthetic data. It is not a validated clinical
multimorbidity score or risk-adjustment model.

## Cost Cohorts

Annual cost cohorts and percentile thresholds are derived from the
synthetic analytical population.

Thresholds such as the 90th or 95th percentile should not be
interpreted as externally validated Medicare high-cost thresholds.

Applying the framework to another population would require
recalculation and validation of the thresholds.

## Member Segmentation

The six member segments are transparent rule-based analytical
constructs developed from patterns observed in the synthetic data.

They are not validated clinical classifications.

They should not be used directly for:

- clinical diagnosis
- medical decision making
- eligibility determination
- coverage decisions
- utilization management decisions
- reimbursement decisions
- automated care-management enrollment

Application to real healthcare populations would require
independent analytical validation, governance, and appropriate
clinical and business review.

## No Causal Interpretation

Relationships identified in this project are descriptive.

For example, greater chronic-condition burden may coexist with
higher utilization and cost, but the analysis does not establish
that one variable caused another.

Similarly, differences between member segments should be
interpreted as associations within the synthetic analytical
population.

## Prescription Drug Interpretation

Prescription Drug Events capture synthetic prescription activity.

Product identifiers, quantity dispensed, days supply, patient
cost, and total prescription cost are used for utilization and
cost analysis.

The project does not assess medication appropriateness,
adherence, treatment effectiveness, or clinical outcomes.

## Agent Limitations

The Medicare Claims Intelligence Agent separates analytical
calculation from language-model reasoning.

Validated DuckDB tools perform numerical calculations.

The local language model performs question routing and answer
synthesis.

The language model can still produce incorrect interpretation,
unsupported wording, or routing errors.

Agent answers should therefore retain:

- analytical provenance
- tool identity
- retrieved knowledge sources
- synthetic-data caveats

when appropriate.

## Retrieval-Augmented Generation Limitations

RAG retrieves relevant documentation from a defined knowledge
corpus.

Retrieval does not guarantee that the highest-ranked chunk fully
answers every question.

A low-quality or incomplete knowledge corpus can lead to incomplete
answers even when retrieval itself functions correctly.

Retrieved documentation should be treated as supporting context;
structured analytical questions should continue to use validated
DuckDB tools for numerical computation.
